Four kinds of phone

Businesses that lose bookings to a ringing phone.

These describe shapes of business, not a client list. No customer work gets named on this site until there is a signed reference to name.

Clinics and practices

Dentists, physios, opticians, veterinary. Reception cannot be with a patient and on the phone at the same moment, and the phone tends to lose.

  • Room, clinician and equipment held together as one booking
  • Recall and follow-up appointments offered inside the same call
  • Anything clinical passed straight to a person, never answered by script
Today

The phone rings while reception is with someone. The caller waits, gives up, and rings the practice down the road instead.

With the assistant

It picks up on the second ring and books into the same diary. The appointment simply appears on the screen behind the desk.

Garages and trades

Plumbers, electricians, garages, mobile mechanics. The person who answers the phone is the same person currently under a van.

  • Answers while both hands stay busy on the job
  • Takes the make, model and symptom, so the day can be planned properly
  • Books a realistic window rather than a promise nobody can keep
Today

Calls go to voicemail all day and get returned in the evening. By then some of those callers have already booked somebody else.

With the assistant

Every call gets answered while the work carries on. Bookings land in the diary, and the evening stays yours.

Salons and studios

Hair, beauty, tattoo, treatment rooms. The busiest hours on the phone tend to be exactly the hours when nobody can reach it.

  • Books by service length, so a colour never lands in a trim slot
  • Holds the right person, where a client asks for them by name
  • Fills a cancellation from the waiting list within seconds of it appearing
Today

The phone rings mid appointment. Answering it is rude to the client in the chair, and ignoring it costs a booking.

With the assistant

It answers, books the right length with the right person, and nobody has to stop what they were doing.

Professional services

Accountants, solicitors, surveyors, consultants. A first call is a lead, and a lead cools by the hour.

  • Qualifies the enquiry before it takes up a fee earner's day
  • Books a consultation into the right person's calendar, not a generic slot
  • Anything sensitive handed to a person rather than handled on a script
Today

An enquiry arrives at six in the evening, gets a callback two days later, and has gone cold by the time anyone speaks to it.

With the assistant

It answers at six, takes the detail, and books the consultation before the caller has closed the laptop.

Signals

Worth a call if any of these sound familiar.

  • The phone goes to voicemail whenever the team is busy, which is most of the day
  • Callbacks happen in the evening, after the caller has already booked elsewhere
  • Nobody knows how many calls came in yesterday, let alone how many nobody reached
  • Bookings get written on paper first and typed in later, whenever a moment appears
  • Evenings and weekends go unanswered entirely
  • An answering service takes messages but cannot see the diary, so nothing gets booked
