    What gets built

    Three things a scheduling system has to get right.

    Constraints, held properly

    Skills, shift patterns, travel time, kit availability, job order, statutory rest. A plan that ignores any one of these looks tidy at eight and falls apart by eleven.

    Rebuild, not restart

    A no-show at nine should reshape what remains and leave the settled part of the day alone. The system recalculates in milliseconds and shows exactly what moved.

    Built to keep pace

    Open data formats, documented rules, standard interfaces. The scheduling logic belongs to the business, so the next tool plugs in and nothing needs rebuilding.

    Method

    How a system gets delivered.

    Four stages. Each one ends with something the business can check.

    Map the real constraints

    Time with the people doing the work, listing every rule the plan must respect, including the ones nobody ever wrote down.

    Model the rules

    Each rule becomes something testable. Where two rules conflict, the business picks the winner and that decision gets recorded, not buried.

    Build and prove

    The model runs against weeks already worked before it touches a live day. If it cannot beat the whiteboard, it does not ship.

    Hand it back with the manual

    Documentation, admin training, and the rule set written in plain English, so the team runs the system without calling anyone.

    Future proof, defined

    A phrase worth pinning down.

    Future proof gets claimed often and tested rarely. Here is what it means at Intelligentside, stated plainly enough to hold us to.

    • Data stays in open formats and exports in full, at any time, with no charge for leaving.
    • Scheduling rules live in readable configuration, not buried inside compiled code.
    • Interfaces follow published standards, so a new tool connects without a rewrite.
    • No lock-in clause. The logic and the data belong to the business that paid for them.
    • Written handover, so a change of supplier costs weeks and not years.
