The phone gets answered. The appointment gets booked.

Intelligentside builds an assistant that picks up the call, answers from your own records, books the appointment into the diary, then handles the confirmation, the reminder and the review request. No queue, no voicemail, no callback list.

What it does

Three jobs, from the first ring to the review.

It answers

It picks up on the second ring, at any hour, and speaks in the words the business would use. Every answer comes from the records the business already keeps, not from a guess.

It books

It reads real availability, offers only times the diary can genuinely hold, then writes the appointment in with the caller's details attached to it.

It follows through

Confirmation by WhatsApp or SMS, an alert to whoever needs it, a reminder call on the day, and a review request once the visit is done.

Diary assignments as text

    Setting it up

    Four steps to a phone that answers itself.

    Each stage ends with something the business can check for itself.

    Point the number at it

    A new number, or a redirect from the one already printed on the van. Nothing else has to change on day one.

    Give it the answers

    Prices, hours, services, directions. Whatever form they already exist in becomes what the assistant says on the phone.

    Connect the diary

    It reads real availability and writes real appointments into the calendar the team already opens each morning.

    Listen to the first week

    Every call becomes a written log. Answers get corrected where they need it, and the assistant tightens from there.

    Future proof, defined

    A phrase worth pinning down.

    Future proof gets claimed often and tested rarely. Here is what it means at Intelligentside, stated plainly enough to hold us to.

    • Call logs, transcripts and booking history export in full, at any time, with no charge for leaving.
    • What the assistant says lives in readable configuration, not buried inside something nobody can inspect.
    • It writes to the calendar and messaging tools the business already pays for, through published interfaces.
    • No lock-in clause. The number, the data and the booking history belong to the business.
    • Written handover, so a change of supplier costs weeks and not years.

    Count the calls nobody picked up last week.

    That number is usually the whole business case. A short call is enough to work it through, and the honest answer is sometimes that you do not need this yet.
